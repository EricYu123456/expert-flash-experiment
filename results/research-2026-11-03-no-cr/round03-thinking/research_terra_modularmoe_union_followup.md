# ModularMoE and nonlinear group union: bounded primary-source review

Source-only follow-up. The proposed original-expert-cohort/page-union objective is exploratory and has no adopted candidate, candidate clock, quality result, or experiment here.

## Judgement

**Do not claim shared nonlinear groups, learned expert masks, or nonadditive MoE union cost as first inventions.** ModularMoE and Mixture-of-Masks share nonlinear units among experts; PCoMoE reuses a source SwiGLU expansion across several Down projections; SpecFit explicitly optimizes a nonadditive expert-union cost. Their inspected methods do not establish the *identical combination* of fixed original expert cohorts, post-trained shared nonlinear GU groups with expert-private Down, and a physical NAND page/plane cost during learning. That is a conditional gap, not proof of publication novelty or viability.

The useful testable question, if a formal candidate is later chosen, is whether a physically accounted cohort-aware objective improves quality at a fixed end-to-end latency/capacity budget over (1) the same group representation and data with per-expert/additive sparsity, (2) route-coactivation/pairwise grouping, and (3) whole-expansion sharing. All controls should use the **same ordinary-loader host or NAND execution choice**, layout, precision, model, quality budget, and training budget. Then test whether the die placement itself adds value relative to that strong ordinary-loader host. If page-union training differs only by an interchangeable regularizer and no physical/quality Pareto improvement survives, this is an integration variant rather than a compelling standalone method.

## What the closest work actually does

* **[ModularMoE, ACL Findings 2026 §§3.2–3.4 and Algorithm 1](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.174.pdf).** It derives a neuron co-activation graph with edge weight `Σ_x |a_i(x) a_j(x)|`, partitions into equal-size, disjoint neuron modules, and constructs a task expert as a *combination* of shared modules. DSelect-k trains a sparse task-level module selection against task model loss (Eq. 12), with an explicit fixed-cardinality selection; after training it binarizes with TopK. Multi-task training uses a static task-conditioned mask/LoRA. It presents the GLU activation in Eq. 9, but the reported §4.1 experiments use ReLU variants of LLaMA2/ChatGLM3. The inspected objective does not encode the union of physical NAND pages under a fixed set of selected original MoE experts. It converts dense pretrained FFNs into task experts; its task experts should not be described as the fixed existing routed-expert cohort. §4.4/Fig. 7 reports 4-bit llama.cpp measurements on Jetson TX2 and Raspberry Pi; this is useful ordinary-hardware evidence, not evidence of multi-expert same-token union compute or NAND timing.
* **[Mixture-of-Masks, ACL 2025 §§2.2–2.4](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.768.pdf).** Learns overlapping expert masks over shared FFN dimensions using L0-regularized sparsification; supports separately masking gate, up and hidden dimensions, and derives shared, independent and unused columns for inference with weight tying. Its Eq. 5 constrains sparsity per expert/layer, rather than the supplied original-expert cohort's NAND page-union cost. It converts dense to sparse experts with continued training; cannot assume pretrained independent experts were compressed with frozen router, or that all selected experts share exact output coefficients.
* **[PCoMoE §§4.1–4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01024v1).** Builds nonlinear expansion-to-Down paths `D_j(U_i(x))`, reuses expansion sources, and uses compatibility-guided path routing. Diagonal paths preserve the original expert structure. It recombines entire expansions, changes path eligibility/routing and fine-tunes, rather than learning neuron-group page unions at a fixed original route. Strong quality/compute baseline with zero new expert weights.
* **[SpecFit §3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15417v1).** The expert set required by speculative draft-token nodes is an explicit monotone submodular coverage union, used in throughput optimization. This rules out generic “union cost for MoE” novelty. Its elements are experts transferred for multiple speculative tokens, not GU neuron pages for one fixed routed cohort; there is no shown NAND group assignment or per-expert student training.
* **[EdgeXpert §IV-C](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05303v1).** For multiple speculative tokens, hardware ordinarily loads the union of requested expert channels. It replaces that union by a salience-selected subset and compensates using already loaded channels, intentionally trading quality for reduced external-memory access. Its cross-token channel coalescing is an important nonadditive hardware/quality prior, not demonstrated physical NAND page sensing or fixed-cohort cross-expert atom sharing.
* **[FloE ICML 2025 §§3.2–3.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.05950v1).** Uses contextual intra-expert sparsification, up-projection quantization/prediction, and compactly colocates gate/down channels to improve DRAM→GPU transfer. It measures bandwidth on that system; it is not an in-NAND or shared-across-existing-experts pool demonstration.
* **[LLM in a Flash ACL 2024 §3.2 and App. D](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.678.pdf).** Bundles corresponding FFN row/column data for contiguous flash reads. Its attempted co-activation closest-friend bundles repeatedly loaded popular neurons and failed the authors' goal. This is a concrete warning against pairwise coactivation scores that ignore duplicate page/atom use. The paper is flash-offloaded dense-model work, not this MoE training objective.

## Precise objective and counterexamples

Let frozen original router produce cohort `S(x)`, static learned assignment `m[e,g]` indicate if expert `e` uses shared nonlinear group `g`, and physical placement map groups to pages. A plausible page-access term is `E_x Σ_p 1[∃ e∈S(x), g on page p : m[e,g]=1]`, plus the expert-private Down, metadata, precision and reliability costs. The representation error must compare the actual router-weighted combined output, not independently approximate each expert and assume errors cancel. The original-router cohort, separate optional shared expert, fixed finite storage, and calibration/holdout distribution all matter.

Equal per-expert group count does not imply equal union: two experts each selecting two groups can select `{a,b}` and `{a,b}` (union 2), or `{a,b}` and `{c,d}` (union 4). Conversely equal union size does not imply equal page accesses: if `a,b` are the requested groups, packing `{a,b}` on one page costs one accessed page, while packing `{a,c}` and `{b,d}` costs two. A NAND **wave** is not necessarily the number of pages: concurrently served planes, chip placement, load imbalance, command rules, partial sums, ECC and output drain determine the elapsed service. Therefore a sum-of-page-indicators may be a starting accounting term, but should not be relabelled measured latency or a correct plane-wave objective without a supported scheduler.

The same observed expert cohort and group masks are available to an ordinary host loader; useful flash caching and coalescing must not be withheld from it. Prevent artificial advantage over source W4/quantized original experts or naive full-expert loading, and pay for group padding, duplicated popular atoms, indices, reconstruction, teacher signals, calibration and private Down. Shared group construction changes pretrained functions unless the original atoms really coincide. No searched result proves either their occurrence or attainable quality.
