# Shared nonlinear atom pool across routed experts: source screen

Source-only exploratory research; no candidate data, model weights, calibration samples, quality bodies, code execution or simulation. This does not start a formal candidate clock or establish global novelty.

## Key finding

Cross-expert sharing of nonlinear hidden neurons is **not new**, nor is reusing a whole SwiGLU expansion with multiple output projections. A potentially distinct, unproven combination is a post-trained pool of paired gate/up atoms shared by selected existing experts, with expert-specific down coefficients and the original live router; only the union of requested atoms is evaluated. To support a systems claim it must preserve acceptable end-to-end quality under matched compression and beat an ordinary-loader host given the **same pool, layout, router and active-set reuse**. It must additionally beat approaches that share whole nonlinear expansions or reduce expert width; merely retaining nonlinearities relative to a failed affine surrogate is not enough.

## Primary sources, read scope and differences

* [LLaMA-MoE, EMNLP 2024 §2.1 and Appendix C](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.890.pdf): starts from a dense SwiGLU FFN and explicitly studies `SharingInner`, assigning a neuron to multiple experts, and `SharingInter`, creating independent shared blocks. Corresponding gate/up/down slices are retained. Its adopted main route and continual pretraining do not demonstrate post-hoc merging of an existing routed expert bank or NAND speedup. Nonetheless, shared SwiGLU neurons among experts are explicit prior art.
* [MoEBERT, NAACL 2022 §3 and §4.5](https://aclanthology.org/2022.naacl-main.116.pdf): shares important neurons across constructed experts and distills. The authors find that sharing **all** neurons hurts performance, attributed to lost expert diversity. BERT/dense-to-MoE is not this SwiGLU/NAND scenario, but is an important concrete counterexample to assuming universal overlap is free.
* [DERN, EMNLP Findings 2025 §§3.1–3.3](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.820.pdf): decomposes *existing MoE experts* into neuron-level `(gate, up, down)` triplets, creates a shared pool of segments from dropped experts, reassigns and clusters them into retained experts. It is offline expert pruning/merging with rerouted retained experts, not runtime exact union evaluation with original router and per-expert down coefficients. It is a strong neuron-alignment and quality baseline, not a direct proof that the proposed runtime already exists.
* [PCoMoE §4.1–4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01024v1): factors an existing SwiGLU expert into nonlinear expansion `U_i` and down projection `D_j`, permits `D_j(U_i(x))` paths, and groups source expansions for reuse. Diagonal paths reproduce the original structure; off-diagonal paths require compatibility routing/fine-tuning, so it is not the identical fixed-router, neuron-granular atom bank. Its runtime reuse of a nonlinear source across several projection destinations is a particularly strong **ordinary compute** prior; zero extra stored weights is a meaningful control.
* [GS-MoE §§3.2–3.5, App. B](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.32009v1): combines groupwise shared linear projections with per-expert intermediate-neuron compression and sensitivity-aware budget allocation. It explicitly observes that a single common subspace cannot reconstruct directions outside it, and reports experts with no routed calibration inputs in a tested architecture. Its shared *linear* basis and within-expert pruning are not the same as a shared nonlinear atom pool. Same total parameters/precision/quality and its nonuniform allocation are strong controls.
* [LorExperts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07814): coactivation communities and local dominant experts with low-rank residuals. The abstract is sufficient for this high-level comparison; no speed or exact dataflow is inferred. Also compare [NimbleMoE](https://openreview.net/forum?id=OLZBdbp1pf), whose full source was previously reviewed in `round02-pairbank/thinking.md`: coactivation-aware clustering, structured compensation and live-router sharing already compete for the same storage/active-read savings. Do not equate these linear sharing mechanisms with nonlinear atom sharing.
* [iliria author design note, symmetry-aligned residual coding](https://github.com/PhilipJohnBasile/iliria/blob/main/docs/PERFORMANCE_THEORY.md): proposes SwiGLU joint-neuron alignment plus prototype/residual compressed storage and, separately, one shared GU base plus expert residual GEMVs. The note itself labels this proposed and not built, and its assumed residual-density upside is not a measurement. It is an engineering prior, not the identical nonlinear-atom union. In particular its broad bit-for-bit permutation claim needs qualification: floating-point Down reductions can change with permutation; undoing the permutation before a fixed reference reduction addresses that issue, subject to the actual backend contract.

## Necessary accounting and counterexamples

Let `φ_j(x)=SiLU(g_j·x)(u_j·x)` be pool atoms, and expert `e` use index set `A_e` and its own output columns `d_ej`. With live router weights, `y=Σ_e α_e Σ_(j∈A_e) d_ej φ_j(x)`; computing `φ_j` once is algebraically possible when inputs and atom parameters truly coincide. Ordinary host code can perform this same deduplication. Combining output coefficients before applying them can change floating-point rounding, dynamic activation quantization or backend order; exactness must be defined against a concrete backend. Replacing different original atoms by common representatives is generally **approximate**, even if aligned rows have high cosine similarity. Joint gate/up behavior and activation/output residuals matter; independent row clustering does not justify a functional equivalence.

For an ideal equal-byte illustration, K experts each have I active atoms, H input/output dimensions, with unique union size `u`. Original three matrices cost approximately `3KIH`; the proposed GU pool and unchanged per-expert Down columns cost approximately `(2u+KI)H`. The ideal ratio is `3K/(K+2u/I)`, excluding metadata, page padding, scales, dispatch, nonlinear calculation, partial accumulators and total model operations. If `u=I`, K=2 gives at most 1.5× and K=8 gives at most 2.4× in this isolated byte model; K=1 with `u=I`, or no overlap `u=KI`, yields no benefit. These are bounds for this explicitly constrained layout, not universal ceilings for other down representations, quantization, caching, or whole-model speed.

Flash granularity can reverse apparent savings: at H=2048 with one byte per weight, a paired full gate/up atom is 4096 bytes, so a 16-KiB page can hold at most four if packed tightly. A scattered dynamic union may require a page per atom rather than one page per four atoms. Plane-wave critical path, route-set co-occurrence, ID list/table size, capacity and placement are required. Page placement and atom padding can be shared by the ordinary-loader baseline. Shared expert, when present, is additional existing work and cannot be counted as magically eliminated.

Quality controls must preserve real router α and select experts under fresh prefill **and decode** contexts; validate group coactivation distribution, rare experts, quality tails and distribution shifts. Compare full nonlinear atom reuse with same-budget per-expert neuron pruning, source-bit quantization, coactivation-aware Nimble-style structured representations, PCoMoE source grouping, and globally or group-shared full-input low-rank methods. If the atom pool needs large training or a dense expert-specific coefficient matrix, count its latency and capacity. A large reduction in nominal atom count without page-wave and end-to-end gains, or marginal quality vs these controls, is a valid reason to veto.
