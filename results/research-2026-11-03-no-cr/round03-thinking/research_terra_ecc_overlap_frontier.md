# Source-only follow-up: ordinary NAND, raw computation and ECC correction

This is exploratory prior/theory research, not a new candidate, clock, benchmark or RTL experiment. No model, weight, activation or evaluation payload was accessed.

## Conclusion

**The core mechanism already has a direct prior:** Intel's [US11513893B2, “Concurrent compute and ECC for in-memory matrix vector operations”](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11513893B2/en), especially Fig. 2 and accompanying description, computes a raw MAC concurrently with checking its input weight, obtains the error-bit location, and corrects the MAC using an activation-dependent power-of-two term before releasing the result. Fig. 4 describes sharing correction hardware between nodes. The concrete examples are SRAM and SECDED/DECTED, with DRAM/resistive memories also discussed; they are not a measured AiF/NAND design. A potential remaining systems question is whether adapting the mechanism to ordinary NAND's 1-KiB multi-error BCH codewords, page buffer, packed Q4_K/Q6_K metadata and actual FFN dependencies can improve over *equally pipelined* correction-before-MAC. Calling raw-dot followed by ECC-location output repair novel by itself would be incorrect. This is a technical prior mapping, not a legal opinion.

## Primary evidence and access limits

1. **Exact dot repair and concurrent schedule:** [Intel US11513893B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11513893B2/en), description around Fig. 2 and Figs. 3–4. Example: 128-bit SRAM weight data and 16 MACs, SECDED or DECTED; it explicitly gives ±activation×2^bit for one error, flags result until correction, and proposes sharing correction hardware. Its one-cycle SECDED illustrations and reported flop/timing examples are not AiF BCH, NAND timing or measured PPA. [EPO publication EP4016304A1](https://data.epo.org/publication-server/rest/v1.2/publication-dates/20220622/patents/EP4016304NWA1/document.pdf) is the same named prior family, not an independent invention.
2. **Existing AiF sequence:** [AiF §4.4](https://doi.org/10.1145/3695053.3731073), mirrored in `paper/aif.txt:779–782,816–817`, says each matrix segment undergoes ECCLite **before** GEMV. It describes BCH correction up to 10 errors per 1 KiB and 6.4 GB/s ECC throughput. That number is not the MAC rate and does not establish raw PB-to-PE bypass, an error-location output ABI, word-granular streaming, or overlap. Input latches are already occupied under its LSB be-enc design. The paper does not prove these are impossible to add, or quantify the area of doing so.
3. **Concrete NAND BCH location hardware:** [Texas Instruments AM261x technical reference manual §1.4.14–15, §13.3.2](https://www.ti.com/lit/ug/sprujb6b/sprujb6b.pdf) documents a NAND-support GPMC and separate Error Location Module, BCH error-location for 4/8/16 bits per 512-byte block, and eight processing contexts. This supports that BCH *location outputs are a real implementable interface*, not that AiF's ECCLite exposes them, that a NAND die itself implements this TI host-controller block, that 10 errors/1 KiB has the same cost, or that MAC repair is built into the TI system.
4. **Related algebraic compute correction, different technology:** [US10452472B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10452472B1/en) describes concurrent dot-product and ECC-related operations using an auxiliary memristor crossbar region. [Georgia Tech MAC-ECC dissertation](https://repository.gatech.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/6989641b-3117-4112-9227-ba829d0e2ef2/content) discusses ECC for RRAM-CIM partial sums. Analog compute-error protection and storage-BCH bit-location repair are distinct; neither is evidence for AiF hardware timing or raw NAND page correctness.
5. **Additional potentially close NVM publication, full primary not verified:** publication WO2026035618A1 was surfaced in a third-party index with paragraphs describing pre-ECC computation and corrective terms. The official [WIPO record](https://patentscope.wipo.int/search/en/WO2026035618) returned access denied, and the attempted Google Patents/EPO primary fetches failed in this bounded screen. Therefore it is a lead for later primary verification only; no claim about its exact scope, NAND applicability or publication content is relied upon here.

## Correctness contract for integer and source-format computation

For known signed integer activation `x_i`, raw decoded integer weight `w̃_i`, true weight `w_i`, and a completed BCH decoder that identifies all affected data bits in its correctable range, form `S_raw = Σ w̃_i x_i` and then `S = S_raw + Σ_i (w_i - w̃_i) x_i`. The error locator, original erroneous bit value/direction, codeword-to-physical-to-logical mapping and activation index are required. For two's-complement 8-bit weights, flipping a non-sign bit changes its weight by `(1 - 2*raw_bit)*2^b`; for sign bit 7 the signed place value is `-2^7`, so its delta is `(1 - 2*raw_bit)*(-2^7)`. Parity-bit errors must not be treated as payload errors. Multiple errors may strike the same weight, group, row or accumulator lane; accumulate their deltas without overflow and preserve the baseline's relevant partial-sum ordering.

For Q4_K/Q6_K, a bit error in a **weight code** can be repaired in the corresponding unscaled integer group dot, provided packed-bit-to-code and group/lane mapping are retained. Errors in Q4_K packed group scale/min, Q6_K group scale, or an FP16 superblock scale are different: a single metadata change affects many products, and `±x_i·2^b` is insufficient. A plausible exact schedule holds the raw per-group integer sums and correction terms until the relevant metadata codeword is confirmed/corrected, then applies the corrected scale/min using the source backend's specified reduction/FMA order. Alternatively replay/recompute the whole affected group. No data-dependent index from corrupt metadata may be used without a safe bounds/replay design. Signed maxima and Q8_K `bsums`, activation quantization and zero-block handling follow the chosen reference, not merely algebraic real-number equivalence. No verified bit-exact implementation is claimed.

ECC fail, read-retry, correction failure/miscorrection, multiple simultaneous error events, invalid status, buffer replacement and numerical nonfinite/overflow need explicit behavior. A later retry must squash/replay every affected speculative partial and dependent work; never release the result based on a tentative clean flag. Matching existing ECC reliability requires the same acceptance/failure checks and fallback, not silently ignoring rare errors. Computing on a full *completed ordinary sensed page buffer* before decode does not require CR-read; no source here establishes that a partially sensed NAND page is safe or usable before the ordinary read completes.

## Timing, resources, and fair control

* The actual raw route must duplicate or fan out page-buffer data into ECC and PE, recover codeword boundaries, preserve protected spare/parity, decode error locations and associate them with speculative accumulators. Provide activation access for repair, direction/bit position, queues and arbitration, correct-group metadata, commit/rollback tags, FIFO/backpressure, and enough state to cover ECC latency. Raw data storage, its lifetime while PE/ECC consume it, and ECC throughput are not free. If the same buffer is reused on next sense, prove it survives until both consumers are done; no native double-buffer is established. ECC and all normal sensing still run.
* The strong baseline is not “ECC all page, then MAC all page.” It may pipeline **per codeword**: once each codeword is corrected, deliver it to an incremental MAC while ECC processes later codewords, provided the same raw-buffer/ports/dependencies are provisioned. As a simplified ideal pipeline with `N` equally sized independent units, ECC service `e` and MAC service `c`, its latency after page sensing is `e+c+(N-1)max(e,c)`; ideal concurrent raw MAC+ECC with zero repair is `N max(e,c)`. The difference is only `min(e,c)` pipeline fill, not `N*e`. This is an illustrative scheduling identity, not an AiF measurement: BCH dependencies, codeword alignment, metadata barriers, output drains and shared resources may prevent either ideal pipeline.
* Compare equal-area, equal-ECC-strength direct per-codeword streaming, same raw/corrected data layout, same MAC throughput and output path; include correction queues and worst-case stalls. Compare **end-to-end** ordinary-host loader as well. Candidate only wins if it shortens the actual critical path or measurably reduces total area/energy under the same reliability and input precision; speculative activity can also raise energy.
* Existing AiF paper gives no ECC-vs-PE stage overlap or lane rate. 28 µs ordinary LSB sensing and 6.4 GB/s ECC must not be added or maximized without an explicit resource timeline and unit of service. One actual error across many pages may arrive late, cross codeword boundaries, or contend with later work; error rates and correlations depend on physical conditions, not an invented independent-error distribution.

This bounded review finds direct general prior plus open implementation questions; it establishes neither a NAND-specific absence of prior art nor the profitability of an AiF implementation.
