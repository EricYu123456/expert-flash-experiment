Why the complete-bipartite condition is exact
--------------------------------------------
Fix one query and one A/B subset. n=nA selected A experts each need eight
identical85274ns jobs, one per distinct chip. F_c is the fixed work from B GU,
all selected private Down, and shared. T must be at least max F_c. Define
  cap_c=min(n,max(0,floor((T-F_c)/85274))).
The explicit network is source->expert capacity8, expert->each chip capacity1,
chip->sink capacity cap_c. Total requested flow is8n. Thus cap_c<=n preserves
the distinct-chip constraint, rather than treating all8n tasks as unrelated.

Necessity: sum cap>=8n. Sufficiency: for every expert subset of size k<=n,
the corresponding generalized Hall/mincut condition is
  sum_c min(cap_c,k)>=8k.
Because0<=cap_c<=n, min(cap_c,k)>=(k/n)cap_c. The total capacity condition
therefore implies every such cut. This is the standard complete-bipartite
b-matching/maxflow argument, not a new theorem. n=0 is a separate case: no
A jobs exist, optimum=max F_c. Merely checking sumcaps>=0 without this fixed
work lower bound would be incorrect; the audited implementation handles it.

full1536B input reload768ns, NAND84000ns, and128nominal4B outputs256ns:

For any legal fixed layout using this A128x8 or frozen B family and unchanged

this relaxation. Each actual chip must do at least its summed owned stages,
after initial host submit. Hence L_q=2000+min_masks min_assignment max_c W_qc
