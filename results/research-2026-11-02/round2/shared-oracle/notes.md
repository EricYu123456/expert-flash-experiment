The shared-GU replication hypothesis has measurable headroom, but it is an application of established expert redundancy and dynamic assignment. DeepSeek-V3 explicitly treats the shared expert as an always-selected heavy-load routed expert during decoding and deploys redundant/shared experts ([technical report §3.4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.19437v2)). The official [EPLB implementation](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/EPLB) duplicates loaded experts and places replicas; [Megatron](https://github.com/NVIDIA/Megatron-LM/blob/main/megatron/core/transformer/moe/shared_experts.py) implements shared-expert overlap. A result here needs a device-specific capacity/dataflow contribution beyond these mechanisms.

This calculation uses all 40 Granite4 Tiny training layers and 32 training prompts ×64 steps. Every source GU expert owns eight whole128-row shards in one of two complementary8-chip halves. Every shard has1536 I8 columns,12 NAND pages and three ordinary28us waves. The fixed shared GU adds one such shard to every chip. This is the existing abstract W8/A8 timing model; actual routes were collected from the Q4_K/Q6_K GGUF model. Native AiF output/scales and a consistent full W8 model are separate unresolved issues.

For a route with nA+nB=6, the fixed shared-copy NAND peak is3×28us×(1+max(nA,nB)). Paired shared replicas reduce it to3×28us×max(4,nA,nB): each pair computes its two adjacent128-row logical intervals either one at each owner or one contiguous256-row GEMV at the less loaded owner. Each logical row executes exactly once, with host row reconstruction; no partial dot product or new context is assumed. Every per-layer extra3MiB shared-GU replica budget also buys two entire routed-expert GU copies at the opposite half. The latter choose their execution owner per route, with peak3×28us×(1+max(3,nA−selectedCopiedA,nB−selectedCopiedB)). All2016 pairs were enumerated separately per layer, optimizing the same training mean.

Summing only these all-ready GU-stage NAND bounds across40layers gives15.167496ms for one shared copy,13.589666ms for paired shared copies,14.601439ms for the two most frequent routed experts, and14.560752ms for the exact best pair. A feasible80-expert global-budget greedy control gives14.552426ms. These are stage bounds and **must not be presented as full-DAG inference latencies or summed with a separately optimized down bound**. Root's independent shared+routed DAG replay is the relevant executable schedule comparison. Its initial best-versus-best three-layer mean gain is4.579277%, with additional stronger geometry controls still required.

The hybrid equal-budget family copies only one original half of shared tiles to its opposite half (eight extra128-row tiles) and copies one entire routed expert's GU (eight extra tiles). Both keep and reuse the original shared tiles. Enumerating both shared directions ×64 routed experts gives14.017664ms, a7.58090% reduction in the GU-stage NAND sum. For every route, both the copied expert owner and the legal shared row assignment are chosen jointly. The best first-three-layer choices are original-shared-halfA plus expert35, A plus expert62, and B plus expert19. Their per-route owner/row witnesses are saved in each layer NPZ; complete native DAG comparison is pending separately.

The full shared-GU replica budget is120MiB across40layers,2.0202% of the full routed+shared raw FFN weight payload. It is7680 extra16KiB pages, and the copied128-row tiles occupy whole64KiB waves, so useful-byte and page budgets agree. Device block reservations and concrete allocation are not supplied by this oracle. The global-budget greedy allocation may be less balanced across chips/layers than the paired layout; it is a feasible payload plan requiring a later physical reservation check. Each128-row routed shard copy goes to the opposite member of its fixed pair; its logical rows are unchanged.

The NAND-only exact best-two control also covers arbitrary partial copies of these canonical routed tiles, under the fixed two-half source geometry and fixed one-copy shared layout. In each half choose a source chip with the fewest distinct copied experts, yielding sets S0 and S1. Replicate those entire expert GU matrices to their opposite half. The new cost8(|S0|+|S1|) is no greater than the original tile-copy count. For every route the original peak is at least1+max(3,nA−selected(S0),nB−selected(S1)); the whole copies attain it by balancing movable experts. With16 extra tile copies, at most two whole-expert copies suffice. This is **NAND peak dominance only**: source/destination channel traffic, command granularity, and ready-expert DAG effects are not covered. It does not apply to non-half GU profiles, arbitrary finer row fragments, cached computations, or copies of the shared expert itself.

The oracle exposes mandatory native costs instead of treating them as free. Ordinary one-copy/shared and routed-copy controls issue64 GU matrix commands, send98304 input bytes over ONFI, return32768 output bytes, and transfer the common1536-byte input once over PCIe. On an unbalanced route, coalesced paired-shared execution issues56 commands and sends86016 ONFI input bytes; output bytes are unchanged. Each command costs250ns and the initial host submission2000ns. Private down inputs, sharedDown's1024-column vector, all GU→host→activation→down dependencies, host slots and channel contention belong to the full DAG and are deliberately absent from these NAND bounds.

No test routes were read. Selecting and scoring on the same training routes is optimistic; this is headroom and strong-control construction, not heldout validation, quality evidence, hardware measurement, or novelty proof.
