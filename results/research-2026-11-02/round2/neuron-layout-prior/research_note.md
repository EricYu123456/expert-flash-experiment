The current constraint is physical placement using existing hardware operations. New sparsity masks, quantization policies, column-skipping operators, fused FFN datapaths, parity decoders and raw-output ABIs are outside scope. Earlier neuron-layout and mixed-precision ideas are retained in `prior_sources.json` only as prior-art and exclusion evidence.

Generic coactivation placement is crowded. [Neuralink/Ripple](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.19274v3) orders neurons for contiguous flash reads. [SLIM](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.09201v1), sections 5.4 and 6.1, already combines fused neuron vectors, page alignment, packing and die distribution, including MoE models; section 7.6 explicitly mentions common-wordline multipane placement. [Swarm](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17803v1), sections 5–6, clusters coactivated KV data and stripes work across devices with replica-aware retrieval. [SPLASH](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23816v1), section 5.5, couples page grouping and plane parallelism, but changes selection. None licenses adding its hardware capabilities to AiF as a placement-only change.

The strongest general formulation is older. [Liu and Wu, 2001](https://ir.lib.nycu.edu.tw/bitstream/11536/30045/1/000172367000003.pdf), definitions 1–6 and section 3, models variable-size items and query frequencies, device capacities, and expected maximum per-device retrieval work. Retrieval time can be a step function of item size. Therefore, selected-expert hypergraphs plus rounded page/wave costs are not by themselves a new formulation. [Query-Log Aware Replicated Declustering](https://repository.bilkent.edu.tr/bitstreams/3e48b3b0-a708-4f57-9eb5-321cbd19928c/download) already selects partial copies under capacity constraints. [Altiparmak and Tosun, 2013](https://faculty.cse.louisville.edu/nihat/papers/TOS2013.pdf) already uses LP and maximum flow for replica retrieval with heterogeneous devices and initial delays. Those are strong baseline abstractions.

What may remain is the joint choice of matrix row partitions and fixed owners, including the real NAND-address and existing-compute costs. The items themselves change when output rows are repartitioned. A useful result would need a physically witnessed layout, a nonzero gap against free-row and arbitrary-subset baselines, and a mechanism beyond enlarging the list of group widths. [PAQ](https://camelab.org/uploads/Main/MJ-ISCA12.pdf) and [Sprinkler](https://camelab.org/uploads/Main/sprinkler-HPCA-MJ.pdf) also make generic physical parallelism and compatible transaction packing established prior art.

`geometry_bounds.py` reads thirteen pinned official configs without downloading weights. Its output is a shape-only arithmetic screen for the existing experiment's 16 chips, four 16 KiB planes and one-byte weights. These are not native-checkpoint deployment claims. No neuron mask, quality result, synthetic routing distribution or end-to-end speedup is generated. Published native formats and model operators still need compatibility checks.

The most useful candidate is [Granite 4.0 H Tiny](https://huggingface.co/ibm-granite/granite-4.0-h-tiny/blob/main/config.json): hidden size 1536, expert intermediate size 512, 64 experts, top-6. Its down matrix has 1536 rows of 512 bytes. A g12 partition gives twelve 128-row shards, each exactly one ordinary four-plane wave. Each expert omits one four-chip group. If a selected set omits every group at least once, maximum chip work is five waves. Equal power-of-two shard widths have a six-wave lower bound from indivisible job sizes. This is a possible phase-level gap, not a measured result or guaranteed static solution.

For complement-group g12, let n_j be the number of selected experts whose omitted group is j. Every chip in group j executes K−n_j waves, so maximum work is K−min_j n_j. With K=6 and four groups, five waves requires all four omitted groups in the selected set. This is a constrained hypergraph coloring/declustering problem, not a new optimization abstraction. A fixed random layout often misses a group. Equal chip capacity is possible because 64×12/16=48 shards per chip. No copies are needed.

Physical page count must be kept separate from chip-wave occupancy. Granite down g16 uses three pages on each of sixteen chips, while g12 uses four pages on each of twelve chips: both read 48 pages per expert. The possible benefit is higher multipane utilization and fewer occupied chip-waves, plus changed command/vector costs; it is not fewer physical page reads. g8 also reads 48 pages but uses two waves per active chip.

The up/gate phase needs a separate partition. For 1024 rows and 1536 columns, one wave holds at most 42 whole rows and two waves at most 85. Eight 42-row and eight 85-row shards hold only 1016 rows. Thus the proposed 85/43 split cannot be called one/two-wave: 43 rows require 66048 bytes. A legal all-chip one/two-wave template needs at least nine two-wave chips; for example 9×85+7×37=1024, costing 25 occupied chip-waves. All timing must use the real ceiling rather than template labels.

[Qwen3.8 Flash Next](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next/blob/main/config.json), hidden 2560 and intermediate 640, is another arithmetic case. g10 makes its up/gate shards exactly five waves, but top-10 gives 100 indivisible five-wave jobs. Their maximum chip load is at least 35 waves, although the fluid byte lower bound is 32. g8 also has a 35-wave ideal maximum, so removing chip-wave slack does not imply a latency improvement. Page counts and job-granularity bounds are separate fields in the JSON.

The minimal real experiment is frozen true routing from the official Granite checkpoint, with no changes to its mask or experts. Compare phase-specific power-of-two learned groups, best existing row templates/free 2×2 swaps, arbitrary fixed subsets, and complement g12. Enforce per-chip capacity and emit legal block/plane/wordline addresses. Count physical pages, occupied chip-waves, maximum per-chip work, commands, input fanout and output transfer independently. Fit only original-train routes, freeze, and validate on a separately authorized unseen corpus. If the new family ties the free-row baseline or any advantage is only an enlarged group search, report that result directly.

The official fixed Q4_K_M GGUF is used only to obtain the real routing trace, following the repository's existing collection convention. Its immutable source is the IBM Granite organization, Apache 2.0. This is not a proposed precision change. The collector pins the existing binary, reruns untraced prefill and decode independently, and compares prompt/generated IDs and output bytes. Smoke collection is 2×16 tokens; the screen is the first 32 original-train prompts at 64 tokens each. No new-model test routes are inferred or fitted. The metadata contains always-active shared FFN layers and SSM tensors, so modeled placement results concern routed FFN service, not full-model inference.
