# New mechanism: broad primary-source screen

Started 2026-11-02 21:18 UTC. Scope: ordinary-read MoE acceleration, with or without shared experts, small AiF modification. This is literature/theory research only: no candidate clock, experiment, model downloads, model weights/activations or candidate code.

## 2026-11-02 21:19 UTC — closest Jacobian and lookup precedents

- [PREP, Input-Aware Expert Pruning for Efficient MoE Deployment, §§4.1, Appendix D](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=nsblPHuc4L) explicitly precomputes each expert output and Jacobian at a shared expansion point, uses their first-order approximation for input-dependent **expert importance** via a CPU Faiss maximum-inner-product index, and retains selected full experts; it does not directly substitute the full vector-valued linearized output for every executed expert. Appendix D tests k=3 K-means hidden-state cluster centers as alternatives to a global hidden-state mean and reports worse downstream generalization for that pruning task, attributing it to calibration overfitting. This is strong direct prior to the proposed centroid/Jacobian machinery, but not conclusive evidence about local low-rank expert output reconstruction.
- [Mixture of Lookup Experts, ICML 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/jie25b.html) uses experts during training that operate on the token embedding rather than arbitrary contextual hidden states; before inference it evaluates and stores outputs by token ID as an offloaded LUT, removing expert execution. This is not a post-hoc surrogate of a normal contextual MoE, and its lookup size/quality does not transfer directly; it precludes broad claims that MoE expert outputs cached as tables and stored externally are novel. [Official code](https://github.com/JieShibo/MoLE).
- PREP and MoLE already cover input-dependent linearized expert selection and flash-resident precomputed expert outputs respectively. The unproven candidate is their composition with **local low-rank reconstruction**, and it must show a better end-to-end quality/physical-read Pareto point than simpler global surrogates, raw low-bit experts and cache policies. No such performance claim follows from the papers.

## 2026-11-02 21:20 UTC — nonlinear cache and low-rank control map

- [LAWS §§4–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.04069v1) explicitly proposes stored model outputs plus Jacobian correction near a cached language-prefix representative, with original-model fallback; it also discusses surrogate libraries, validity radii and low-rank diffusion extensions. This is a conceptual preprint, not evidence of a working AiF MoE implementation or meaningful model-accuracy/physical-latency result. Its stated bounds rest on nontrivial smoothness and input/embedding assumptions; do not treat its certificates as automatically valid for W8A8 FFNs or multi-token contexts. Its direct cached-output + tangent + fallback idea substantially narrows novelty.
- [FastCache §§3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20353v1) uses a learned affine surrogate and reuse for transformer diffusion timestep computation, not autoregressive MoE storage. [Neuron-Level Fuzzy Memoization in RNNs](https://upcommons.upc.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/b0a14faa-0e9f-4151-bbff-66616382c287/content) finds that input similarity alone can be misleading, since weight sensitivity matters, and proposes a small binary predictor for approximate reuse. These are strong controls for a distance-only centroid gate, though workloads and hardware differ.
- [SPICE §4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21240v2) fits one global low-rank `B_e A_e x` to the residual between each expert and an always-active shared expert, with full-expert fallback. Candidate without a native shared expert can use a direct global low-rank affine output surrogate or an existing selected expert as a base; these are controls we propose, not implementations disclosed by SPICE. [MiLo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02658) retains a low-bit expert base and adds compact low-rank corrections with adaptive rank; its rank choice is a model compression allocation, not evidence of per-token tangent selection.
- A local-centroid method cannot claim novelty from affine piecewise functions, Jacobians, nearest-neighbor cache, low-rank experts or fallback separately. The candidate-specific falsifiable question is whether an input/route-dependent nonlinear selection among local low-rank expert-output views achieves **better held-out generation quality at the same active NAND waves and total retained bytes** than one global affine low-rank map, W4 full experts, reduced-K, and SPICE-style residuals (where applicable), including finite host DRAM and controller/flash transfer costs. A stronger global baseline can spend all `C` local maps' storage budget on a larger single rank rather than comparing only identical per-map rank.

## 2026-11-02 21:23 UTC — shared projections are prior art; scope and an unresolved lead

- [MoLAE §§4–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.23100v2) explicitly converts pretrained MoE weights into a common projection and expert-specific latent operations, preserving expert nonlinearity; shared low-dimensional input projection across live experts is therefore not a new claim. [ITC-MoE §3.3 and Appendix A.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.01296v1) uses token-aware low-rank compensation and expert-shared Tucker input/output projections in a diffusion LM; both motivate a nonlinear latent expert baseline rather than only a weak globally affine baseline. [MoBE §§2–3 and Appendix B](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=8RV6H50OSf) discusses challenging effective-rank/storage tradeoffs on deployed MoEs and compares low-rank systems. Rank of a weight matrix does not by itself determine rank of the local input-output function: these sources identify risk, not a proof that this proposal fails.
- Important lead, **not verified from the primary full text**: [EBC-LLM, IEEE IVMSP 2026, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/IVMSP68335.2026.11618864), publisher [record](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11618864), is inaccessible to this search tool because of a JavaScript challenge. Search indexing surfaces an abstract that says nonlinear MoE experts are linearized by ridge regression and clustered under a shared rotation, but we have not verified if the clustering is experts, input states, or both; do not cite this as a verified technical result or rule out the candidate from the unverified abstract. Obtaining publisher full text/authors' accepted manuscript is high-value follow-up.
- A runtime centroid guard only knows compressed `z=P_l x`, router state and its metadata before it chooses the read. Distance in `z` does not bound error in omitted input directions or autoregressive distribution shift. A fallback is not a guarantee unless an independently justified bound or held-out calibrated predictor is demonstrated. Any fallback-rate and expert error must be measured jointly, including cost of originals retained on flash.
- A guard can also examine **full layer input** `x`, router IDs/scores, and small host-resident metadata if explicitly budgeted; it need not be restricted to `z`. Examples include full-input norm, projection residual norm for an orthonormal projector, a small learned risk classifier, and precomputed per-view summaries. These are proposed signals, not certified error bounds. A selective guard is evaluated on the cases it accepts; ordinary marginal calibration does not automatically imply coverage for that selected subset. [Selection-conditional conformal analysis](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssb/article/87/4/1239/8113856) explains why post-selection changes the reference distribution; [Conformal Risk Control](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=33XGfHLtZg) and [LLM configuration shift](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01460) give potential baselines/limitations. Neither gives a free deterministic per-expert bound, full-sequence quality guarantee, or flash-speed result. Separate fitting, guard selection and held-out calibration, and characterize dependence/shift before invoking statistical validity.
- A true W8A8 FFN includes data-dependent activation scales and rounding, including after SwiGLU. A Jacobian or smooth Taylor bound from a floating-point surrogate does not automatically apply to this quantized reference; the quantized graph has rounding discontinuities and data-dependent scaling. Evaluate actual teacher outputs and quantify errors rather than calling this numeric exactness.

## 2026-11-02 21:24 UTC — one-read shared-projection hypothesis and information floor

Let a layer project `z=P_l x` once in the host. For each selected expert `e`, select a region using available pre-read information and fetch one affine view `A_{e,c} z+b_{e,c}`. All experts receive the same `z`; the actual native router weights multiply the corresponding approximate outputs. Originals may be retained as fallback. Assuming an ordinary-read GEMV for each view, the critical path can drop the original gate/up followed by down dependency. This is an architectural hypothesis, not evidence that AiF's published command accepts the small vector, that a physical implementation fits, or that quality survives.

**Unavoidable information collision:** if two valid expert inputs `x1,x2` have `P_l x1=P_l x2` and induce the same region/guard state, then every deterministic output built solely from `z` and that state is identical for both. Hence its worst expert-output error on the pair is at least `||E_e(x1)-E_e(x2)||/2` in any norm. More centroids and a more accurate Jacobian cannot overcome this. The joint distribution of routed inputs and selected-expert outputs determines whether such collisions matter in practice; PREP's K-means failure is related but not decisive. Compare one shared `P_l` with expert-conditioned projectors at the same host DRAM, pre-read host time, flash waves, and total storage. Expert-conditioned host projectors are a stronger control, not something to exclude by assuming they are prohibitively large.

**Where the conditional bank might actually add expressivity:** For each expert, `A_{e,c} z+b_{e,c}` can use a different rank-r output subspace per selected region. A global `A_e phi_e(z)+b_e`, however nonlinear `phi_e` is, has all outputs in a *single* affine subspace of dimension at most r. Thus one can construct functions whose conditional output differences span up to `min(Cr,H)` dimensions and which a single rank-r decoder cannot represent exactly. This is a mathematical distinction, not evidence that real MoE outputs exhibit it or that the construction is novel. A larger-rank global nonlinear decoder must be compared at the **same cohort-level physical waves**, not the same rank. The flash page floor can make higher global rank effectively free for an isolated expert: with 16 chips and four 16 KiB planes per chip, an H=2048 W8 `H×64` map is 16 pages if laid out one 8 KiB shard per chip, whereas an `H×512` map is 64 full pages and could fit one ideal 64-plane sense wave for a *single* expert if the matrix can be partitioned into complete output rows and the input/command contract supports it. But six routed experts need 96 versus 384 pages respectively, with ideal aggregate lower bounds of two versus six waves before conflicts/overhead; these bounds do not guarantee an achievable schedule. A bank can therefore benefit from active cohort sparsity even when a single-expert comparison appears tied, and a fair global-rank baseline must exploit any real spare planes.

In particular, under that same 16-chip illustrative row sharding, a global `H×128` W8 map has 16 KiB per chip and therefore exactly the same 16-page footprint per expert as the padded `H×64` local view; it uses twice the input/projector dimensions but still far less bank storage than C=16. Hence the appropriate sense-equal baseline is at least global rank 128, not rank 64, with both host costs and physical command capability accounted for. Page packing, inter-expert scheduling, compression and ECC can change the result, so the equal-footprint statement is conditional on this explicit layout.

For Flash-Jacobian's unrestricted local factor baseline, placing each region-specific `B_{e,c}` in host memory also permits a one-NAND-map read after a region is chosen, at the cost of expert/region-specific host projection and much higher host storage. Example solely for arithmetic: H=2048, r=64, C=16, E=64, 26 routed layers, one byte per coefficient gives `26×64×16×2048×64 = 3.25 GiB` for the B factors alone, plus the same amount for A on flash, bias, scale, keys and alignment. A common `P_l` is about `26×2048×64 = 3.25 MiB` before its metadata. Real host precision, compression, unselected expert residency, launch, bandwidth and selection cost are unresolved; no assumption that 3.25 GiB is universally unaffordable. Compare both options on a joint host/flash capacity and latency frontier; this is the concrete reason a shared-P design could matter rather than a claim of a new local fitting method.

**Calibration occupancy illustration:** with N=30,000 layer inputs, uniform K=6 of E=64 and C=16 balanced regions, mean training occupancy is only `N K/(E C)≈176` per expert-region. At C=32 it is about 88. For r=128, the per-region affine fit has 129 scalar input coefficients per output, so 88 observations are necessarily underdetermined; ridge regularization does not create missing information. Routing skew and uneven regions can make the lower tail much smaller than this mean; shared training examples are not independent merely because they are separate token rows. This is a parameter-count check, not evidence about the actual route distribution or model quality. Report occupancy per expert and region, condition numbers, effective fit degrees of freedom, train/validation prompt split, teacher precision, and a meaningful low-occupancy fallback. Global/full-hidden-state input regression can be more underdetermined still.

The serious control is a shared-`P` *nonlinear* latent expert, `A_e phi_e(z)+b_e`, with its small nonlinear transform stored in host memory or included in its physical read budget. It has the same single large `H×r` output map as the proposed local view, and can evaluate a nonlinear function on `z` without buying `C` flash-resident copies. Compare also per-region ridge/OLS (no Jacobian), global higher rank, MoLAE/Nimble-style nonlinear latent experts, W4 native experts, original lower-K, and native-shared SPICE. Rank, P, fit/training budget, retained originals, effective memory capacity and route/fallback traces must be matched; requiring identical rank alone is not enough.

**Illustrative logical byte accounting only:** H=2048, I=1408, W8 gives an original expert `3HI=8,650,752 B` (8.25 MiB). A hypothetical W8 `H×64` view is 131,072 B (0.125 MiB), plus bias, scale, encoding and alignment. Sixteen views require 2 MiB/expert, and original-plus-bank 10.25 MiB/expert before metadata/ECC, vs a 4.125 MiB W4 original before scale. Existing AiF model has 16 chips × 4 planes × 16 KiB/page and 28 μs ordinary be-encoded LSB page read; this does not by itself give candidate throughput. At 16-chip row sharding each local view has 8 KiB/chip, so padding and under-filled 16 KiB pages matter; unused planes do not imply bytes were actually read. Multiple experts, smaller chip groups, unequal plane occupancy, channel contention, input posting, command dimensions and fallback must be scheduled fairly. `r=64` bytes or its padding/encoding as an AiF operand is **not established**; paper says input is typically 8–32 KiB, and numeric wire format/minimum vector width are not disclosed ([AiF notes](aif_model_notes.md)).

The AiF command does include a vector-dimension operand, so do not assert that short vectors are unsupported or automatically require a new die. Padding only the host vector may be adequate if a command can describe an `H×r` physical matrix and discard padded inputs; its exact operand and byte-stride contract is undisclosed. Conversely, if an implementation forces physical `H×H` matrix padding and must read all those zero weights, its sensing benefit can vanish. Zero extents represented logically in a driver are not proof they are skipped by a real PE/NAND command. Keep these as explicit alternative hardware contracts until independently verified.

## 2026-11-02 21:25 UTC — fold-first precedents and joint-anchor scope

- **Strongest near-direct algorithmic prior found during formal-candidate screen:** Anonymous ACL submission [Linear Access in LLM Representations, §§3.1–3.2 and Appendix C](https://openreview.net/attachment?id=au5FGRgwcZ&name=pdf), calls its method *Flash-Jacobian*: clusters LLM hidden inputs, stores one low-rank operator per region `J_k=A_k B_k`, and predicts `y≈mean(y)_k+J_k(x−mean(x)_k)`; it fits conditional least-squares operators with ridge/alternating updates. The authors explicitly say these are population conditional linear predictors, **not** exact pointwise Jacobians. Their `Flash` names a GPU-efficient fitting technique and **is not NAND in-flash computation**. Main goal is mechanistic audit, not ordinary-read latency or whole-model autoregressive replacement. It stores a **separate input factor** per region; no layer-shared input projection across all active MoE experts is established by the cited section. Thus shared-P, expert routing, finite NAND scheduling and full-quality question remain distinguishable, but claims that clustered local low-rank affine LLM operators are novel are untenable. The anonymous manuscript is public; exact submission date/peer-review outcome are not verified here.
- Appendix C Table 34 reports on an isolated Qwen-3.5-4B L12 MLP that 64-cluster low-rank ridge attains cosine 0.096 / relative L2 0.997 **on held-out exact differential JVP**, compared with diagonal 0.475 / 0.880. This measures derivative-vector approximation, not direct FFN output, not an MoE expert, and not generation quality; it is a serious Jacobian-fidelity warning, not evidence that a direct local-output regression bank fails. Appendix C.35 additionally warns more regions reduce per-region training occupancy, so fit/center sample counts and disjoint prompts are essential. The paper's ordinary centered-input/output conditional ridge provides the key secant/OLS control.
- **Very close contextual guard prior:** [Half the Nonlinearity Is Wasted §§3.1–3.2, 4.3.4, 5.8](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03459v1) fits a linear substitute for a full MLP, trains a lightweight gate on the contextual input to choose the original MLP or its surrogate, and tests the choice across GPT-2 and Pythia. For fitting, the classifier target is the change in downstream per-token cross-entropy after replacing the MLP, not merely its local reconstruction error; this distinction can make it a particularly strong guard control. In its GPT-2 Medium test with 20 K-means input clusters after PCA to 50 dimensions, the clusters were poor predictors of whether a **single global** linear model was safe (AUC 0.519–0.611 at three layers; no clean clusters). That result does not test one independently fitted linear map per cluster and does not use MoE expert-conditioned or gated-SwiGLU FFNs, so it must not be misstated as refuting local-bank reconstruction. It is nonetheless a stronger direct warning and baseline than generic nearest-neighbor caching. The paper reports no optimized wall-clock speedup and does not study AiF. [Author code](https://github.com/pbalogh/half-the-nonlinearity) is available for inspection, not exercised here.
- [TARDIS §§5.1–5.4, 9](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.10054v1) has **a folded affine matrix for a full LLM FFN**, neuron-wise range fitting, a low-bit predictor, original-neuron correction and an implemented vLLM/GPU path. §5.1 identifies the exponentially many combinations of separately chosen neuron ranges; §9 discusses the gated FFN difficulty. The present idea chooses a small number of **joint input-space regions**, not all per-neuron range combinations. TARDIS does not establish that a practical bank of shared-projection views wins, but does defeat novelty claims for folding, predicting outliers or approximating a nonlinearity to eliminate an FFN stage. Compare against a single folded map plus its predictor and fair correction cost. I found no verified source yet establishing that TARDIS already implements the same joint-anchor low-rank MoE bank; absence in this search is not evidence of patentability.
- [How Linear Is a Transformer Feed-Forward Block?](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19379v1) directly studies least-squares reconstruction of FFN blocks and the difference between high local reconstruction metrics and downstream perplexity, with material depth/model dependence and optimization caveats. Its experimental scope is not a direct demonstration for dynamically selected MoE expert views. This strengthens the need for full-model held-out quality and well-conditioned ridge/OLS, not only an expert-output cosine/MSE metric.
- [Large Language Models are Locally Linear Mappings §§2.2–2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24293v1) constructs input-dependent effective linear maps via the nonlinear operations' frozen coefficients. These maps reproduce a given forward pass under the construction, but are **not automatically the true Jacobian**, and do not by themselves establish accuracy at neighboring inputs. Avoid citing the title as a local Taylor guarantee.
- Cross-domain precedent: the author-hosted thesis [Analytic Treatment of Deep Neural Networks, §3.2](https://repository.kaust.edu.sa/bitstreams/fb0928c9-fe73-4065-9bcd-6826ad7d752e/download) clusters inputs, constructs multiple local two-stage linearizations of a teacher, distills per cluster and learns a gate, including shallow nonlinear local functions. Its tested setting is not an AiF autoregressive MoE. Together with classic hierarchical [mixtures of linear experts](https://bitsavers.trailing-edge.com/pdf/mit/ai/aim/AIM-1440.pdf), it establishes that joint clusters/local regression/selective linearized surrogates are old in general, even if the present deployment combination is not directly identified.

## 2026-11-02 21:25 UTC — shared-branch accounting and decision conditions

For the **specific DeepSeek-V2-Lite dimensions** (H=2048, routed I=1408, K=6, one full shared branch I=2816), routed plus native shared weights in the original W8 FFN total `6×8.25 + 16.5 = 66 MiB` of logical weights per token and routed layer. Six selected rank-64 W8 views plus the unchanged native shared branch would instead logically request `6×0.125 + 16.5 = 17.25 MiB`, before padding/scales and fallback. These are algebraic byte totals, **not** predicted service times or speedups: the branch can overlap, 4-plane tail occupancy/packing matters, and attention, embedding, CPU/key computation, output transfers and dense layer remain. The OLMoE configuration has no native shared FFN; both must be evaluated. Allow the best implementation of the baseline to schedule all branches as well. Whether to approximate the native shared branch is a separate quality/cost choice, not a requirement for candidate generality.

**Recommended single question for a formal 30-minute thought review, contingent on root selection:** Can a layer-shared pre-read projection plus a flash-capacity-bounded choice among locally fitted affine expert views collapse the **gated** FFN dependency while preserving held-out end-to-end quality at the same measured physical page waves, host DRAM/time, and total flash budget as (1) W4 full experts, (2) optimally fitted global affine and nonlinear latent models, and (3) TARDIS-like folded/corrected alternatives? The informative scientific variable is whether *joint contextual selection* buys quality at fixed physically active work, versus a global small nonlinear model. The low-dimensional information-collision result and finite one-page/plane granularity give direct ways to disprove the premise. If the local bank wins only against global linear or W8 but not equally fast nonlinear/W4 controls, or its finite-resource guard has low coverage, reject as a compilation of existing components. This recommendation is to think rigorously about one candidate, **not** approval to run a probe or evidence of novelty/speed/quality.

Highest-value further prior source is the inaccessible full EBC-LLM paper; highest-value non-experimental feasibility information is the actual AiF command/operand numerical encoding and whether native vector length can express the low-rank input. If EBC-LLM or another primary source discloses the complete input-selected bank and conditional fallback, re-evaluate the candidate's novelty. No model weights, activations or candidate probes were used in this screen.

## 2026-11-02 22:00 UTC — exact row-packing arithmetic versus AiF disclosures

The draft uses output H=2048, raw one-byte W8 coefficients, 16 KiB data pages, and groups of chips. With full logical rows and no extra metadata, r64/g2 gives 1024 rows/chip ×64 B = 65,536 B = four pages/chip, 256 complete rows/page; r128/g4 gives 512 rows/chip ×128 B = four pages/chip, 128 rows/page. These are **layout arithmetic**, not a claim that AiF firmware implements or can issue the schedule. A hypothetical equal four-plane split on each chip uses four parallel pages in each chip; it must still establish the plane addressing/partitioning, page-buffer traffic, ECC, output FIFO, and completion dependencies. Multiple active experts, channel sharing, row-scale bytes and input posting consume resources. No bytes for omitted/zero padding are free unless the native command/physical layout actually omit them.

The paper's `aif_post` specifically advertises equal submatrices distributed to *all* AiFChips, with GEMV carrying input and page-range parameters. Grouped g2/g4 placement needs at least a changed host/controller placement policy and corresponding chip-subset dispatch; whether the exact published flash command already supports the desired subset and per-plane whole-row packing is not established. This is a **firmware/controller contract to specify**, not demonstrated evidence that a new die or page-buffer change is necessary. Holding selected-expert collision patterns constant, the same grouped placement must be offered to global and other control maps.

The input is loaded to PB after **each described GEMV command**; a single command reuses that input as matrix pages stream. Neither a persistence guarantee across distinct GEMV commands nor a no-cost broadcast/reuse primitive appears in the cited paper description. That absence is not proof a controller could not add one; compare explicit post-per-command against a separately specified shared-input/context-retention design. `aif_post` and `aif_gemv` have no disclosed fixed numeric launch latency; launch, DMA/channel serialization, SREG FIFO polling and host work cannot be assigned measured values from this paper alone.

§4.4 specifies that generated output elements **stream through an output FIFO**; the status register (SREG) tracks FIFO status while the controller polls round-robin. It does not say the entire output vector is stored in SREG. Thus r128/g4's 512 output elements per active chip are not forbidden just because an SREG/FIFO cannot buffer 512 entries simultaneously; actual FIFO depth, element width, poll protocol, drain throughput and backpressure costs remain unspecified and require a disclosed implementation or sensitivity sweep. Their differences versus a 16-chip baseline are charged in the same interface model.

Physical-plane feasibility is device-specific. [ONFI 5.2, §§3.1.1, 7.1, 7.6](https://onfi.org/files/ONFI_5_2_Rev1.0.pdf) says multi-plane read is optional, its constituent operations address distinct planes on the same LUN, and some devices require matching page addresses and impose additional block-address restrictions. A candidate one-wave layout must satisfy the selected target's actual rules and reserve compatible page locations; an unconstrained per-plane page-count model alone cannot establish this. Concurrent multi-plane commands wait for their address/command sequence before array service; individual command/address cycles and any vendor busy requirements are overhead. ONFI's `Change Read Column Enhanced` rule governs external data output and does **not**, by itself, prove the on-die AiF PE's page-buffer data path requires an external data transfer. The AiF paper does not identify a precise ONFI revision/device contract for the hypothetical chip; this is a sensitivity constraint, not a blanket rejection of its four-plane model.

The paper's 6.4 GB/s has **two distinct uses**: it is the ECCLITE correction throughput per AiFChip and the advertised effective internal weight-read bandwidth with be-enc **and CR-read**. It is not a measured standalone PE-compute throughput, is not automatically the ordinary-read bandwidth, and is not a license to add `bytes/6.4 GB/s` sequentially after a read if ECC pipelines with reading. Conditional sanity bound: if at 200 MHz the system really accepts 6.4×10^9 useful INT8 weight bytes/s and performs one multiplication per byte, aggregate PE rate must sustain 32 corresponding byte-multiplications/cycle; this is an inference under that throughput assumption, not a disclosed lane count. A finite-command pipeline fill/drain time and partial-page PE utilization remain unknown. For ordinary 28 μs read per full page wave, a 65,536 B/chip group shard has an ECC service lower bound of 10.24 μs/chip at 6.4 GB/s if all those bytes require correction, but actual time depends on overlap, extra bytes and PE throughput.

**Quantization ABI is a separate issue from the number of MAC lanes.** The paper names INT8 multipliers and an adder tree, but the referenced passage does not specify signed encoding, dot accumulator width/rounding, FIFO element encoding, transfer width, row-scale multiplication, or bias application. One *possible* contract would stream exact signed dot products to the host, which applies the row scale, dynamic input scale, and F32 bias, and account for all output bytes/CPU work. For the draft's `[-127,127]` inputs and weights the ideal absolute integer dot bounds are `r·127²`: 2,064,512 at r128 and 8,258,048 at r512, both fit signed int32; the necessary end-to-end encoding and support remain to be established. This does not imply FP32 canonical arithmetic or F32 intermediate bias is natively on the die.

**Row metadata size illustration:** per-view F32 bias plus per-row F32 scale costs `8·H` bytes per expert/region, excluding pointers and ECC. For Deep's 26 routed layers, 64 experts, 32 regions, H=2048, this is 832 MiB; OLM's 16 routed layers at the same E/C/H require 512 MiB. Actual storage placement may be host, flash or shared/quantized depending on the declared format; these sizes are before alignment or compression and must be added to the relevant finite budget, not automatically charged as extra sensing or assumed free. Identical maps/scales/biases may be deduplicated only when the realized values and routing semantics actually match.

Shared-basis precedent is stronger than a generic global low-rank citation. [SLBF §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09342) preserves expert identities and routing while constructing richer shared low-rank weight bases; it is a strong weight-compression control and does not establish the localbank cannot win on ordinary-read waves. For a competitive `U`, plain SVD of concatenated coefficient matrices weights latent directions uniformly and can be a weak strawman. [Reduced-rank regression (Izenman, 1975)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0047-259X(75)90042-1), [MoLAE Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.23100v2) and [Swift-SVD, ICML 2026](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/qi26k.html) motivate fitting bases to actual input/output covariance; [Swift-SVD author code](https://github.com/hiahei/Swift-SVD) is available. The frozen Stage 0 R1 global control separately tunes its pooled eta before local lambda and already has region-dependent biases; report selected eta and finite/infinite lambda explicitly rather than comparing against an untuned global slope.

A precise linear-control construction, derived here rather than claimed as an AiF result: index expert/region groups by `j`; center each group's row-wise training data `Z_j` and `Y_j` using its nonnegative weights `W_j`, so each group gets a free output intercept. For fixed per-group ridge penalties `λ_j`, define `C_j=Y_jᵀW_jZ_j` and `G_j=Z_jᵀW_jZ_j+λ_j I`. In real arithmetic, with one common orthonormal output basis `U` and unconstrained group-specific latent maps `B_j`, minimizing `Σ_j ||Y_j−Z_j B_jᵀ Uᵀ||²_W + λ_j ||B_j||²_F` profiles each `B_j` as `Uᵀ C_j G_j⁻¹`. The optimal q-dimensional U spans the leading eigenvectors of `Σ_j C_j G_j⁻¹ C_jᵀ`; singular G needs an explicitly defined pseudoinverse/ridge rule, and any group/route weights and objective normalization must be frozen. This directly incorporates distinct group input covariances. It is only optimal for that real-valued separable squared-loss model: W8/A8 rounding, finite arithmetic, on-device rank/quantization choices, constrained/interconnected parameters, and a loss on the *weighted sum of simultaneous experts* invalidate the guarantee and require deployed reevaluation and, for the latter, cross-expert error terms. Group-specific vs layer-shared U, nonlinear B and caching are separate Pareto controls.

An oracle lower bound for a **fixed** orthonormal common U and fixed contextual expert biases is `|| (I−UUᵀ)[y_teacher−Σ_e α_e b_e,c] ||²` on each input: no latent function `h(x)`, including an oracle nonlinear one, can make `U h(x)+Σ_e α_e b_e,c` closer in squared norm than this projection residual. Averaging over any nonnegative fixed evaluation weights preserves the inequality. Fit U and biases on FIT, freeze, then evaluate on VALID or fresh holdout to estimate this fixed-model subspace limitation. It is not a lower bound over all alternative learned U/bias choices, and route-dependent `α_e` plus leaf-dependent `b_e,c` themselves contribute varying output directions. The result also does not lower-bound a bank that changes U by context. This test separates insufficient common output subspace from insufficient latent model, without claiming quality or running data here.

The [Swift-SVD author repository README](https://github.com/hiahei/Swift-SVD) says its perplexity evaluation replaces original weights with the reconstructed dense product `A_k B_k` to avoid model-specific forward changes; deployment with explicit factors requires adapting forward execution. Thus it provides an accessible fitting/quality reference, **not** ready-made evidence of factorized B1 AiF latency or memory traffic. Do not benchmark the reconstructed dense path as if it were compressed runtime.

The two-step pooled prior and conditional shrinkage should not be the only statistical reference.
